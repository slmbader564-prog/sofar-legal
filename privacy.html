Privacy Policy: Sofar

Last updated: 3 October 2026

Sofar keeps your life atlas on your phone. There is no Sofar account, no sign-in, no analytics, no advertising, no crash reporting and no tracking SDK. Logging and viewing your places work offline. A few things can leave your phone: place-name lookups you can switch off, Google Play for the optional purchase, and the backups, exports and shares you choose to make. Two optional features, Stays and Memory echo, use your location in the background, and only after you turn them on. Each is described below.

Information we collect

Sofar's developer collects nothing from the app. The app has no server of its own: it does not send your atlas to any server run by us, and we run no server that stores it. Your atlas stays on your phone unless you choose to back it up, export it or share it. The place-name lookups described below send the position of one place at a time, or the words you type in search, never your atlas. If you email us, we receive what you choose to send.

Information stored on your device

To build your atlas, Sofar saves the following in its private storage on your phone:

  • Your places and visits: coordinates and how accurate each reading was, dates and times, names, street, city and country, categories, notes and tags. This includes places you log, places you add by hand and places you import from photos.
  • New places beyond the free allowance, whether you logged or imported them. They are kept with all their data as ghost pins, outside your stats, and join your atlas if you unlock Sofar Complete.
  • For each visit imported from a photo, a reference to that photo in your library and the time it was taken. Sofar keeps the reference, not a copy of the photo.
  • Statistics, medals, trips and streaks computed from your own places.
  • Your preferences, and whether you have unlocked Sofar Complete.
  • An installation identifier that the app uses to recognise its own backups.
  • Nearby place names received from Photon, kept for up to thirty days (see Place names).
  • If you turn on Stays or Memory echo, the information they need to work, described under Location.
  • If you add the home-screen widget, the images it shows.
  • Backup copies of your atlas, kept in the app's private storage and, if you choose one, in a folder you pick.

Backups and exports

Sofar makes a backup copy of your atlas by itself when you open the app, if something has changed and its newest copy is at least 20 hours old. It also makes one just before a restore, and whenever you tap Back up now in Settings. It keeps its newest seven copies, plus the two most recent copies made before a restore, in its private storage, and removes older ones. If you choose a backup folder, Sofar also writes each new copy there. That folder can belong to a cloud storage app, which then syncs it under its own policy. Sofar never deletes files in a folder you chose, and Stop using folder leaves the copies already there.

You can also export your places from Settings at any time as JSON, GPX or CSV, and send the file to an app you choose. The JSON file is the complete backup; GPX and CSV are for other apps and do not restore an atlas. Backup and export files are ordinary readable files that Sofar does not encrypt. They can contain your coordinates, dates, notes, visits you moved to the trash, ghost pins, photo references, your preferences and the installation identifier, so keep them somewhere you trust. When you restore from a backup, Sofar first saves a copy of your current atlas, then adds what the file holds, recognising places and visits that are already on your phone so they are not doubled.

Sofar turns off Android's cloud backup for its own data, so Android does not copy it to your Google account; a phone maker's device-to-device transfer may still copy it.

Location

When you tap Log, in the app, on the home-screen widget or on the quick settings tile, Sofar reads your phone's location, and for up to a minute afterwards it listens for a more accurate reading of the same spot. It also reads your location once when you ask it to use your current position while adding a place by hand. Each place you add keeps its coordinates and date, so your atlas is a record of the places and moments you chose to keep. Sofar does not record where you go between them.

Two optional features use your location in the background: Stays and Memory echo. Both are off until you turn them on. During setup, Sofar offers them on one page with both already ticked, and nothing starts unless you tap Turn on and allow what Android asks for; you can tap Not now instead. You can turn either one on or off at any time in Settings, under Suggestions. Both need Android's permission to use location all the time, and notifications; Sofar explains why before Android asks.

Memory echo: Sofar gives Android a short list of your own places, and Google Play services watches for your arrival at one of them, even while Sofar is closed, so your phone can show you a notification. It does not add a visit. Sofar stores the time of each such reminder so it does not repeat for the same place within 30 days. When you turn Memory echo off, Sofar asks Android to forget the list.

Stays: your phone's activity recognition, provided by Google Play services, tells Sofar when your phone has been still for about twenty minutes. Sofar then takes one location reading in the background and compares it, on your phone, with a copy of your places' positions that it keeps for this purpose. If the spot is not in your atlas yet, you get a notification asking whether to add it. A reading that produces no suggestion is discarded, and Sofar keeps no record of your activity. While a suggestion waits for your answer, Sofar keeps its spot and the time you arrived. It remembers a spot it suggested for seven days so it does not ask again, and a spot you answer Not this one is never offered again. Nothing is added unless you tap the suggestion. Stays is quiet from 22:00 to 08:00 and makes at most two suggestions a day. It also needs Android's physical activity permission.

Photos

Photo access is optional. If you allow it, Sofar reads the location and date stored in your photos to suggest places, and shows small previews from your library. To find the date, it reads the first 64 KB of each photo file, and the whole file only when that is not enough. All of this happens on your phone. Sofar keeps a reference to each photo a visit came from, not a copy, and it does not upload your photos. While photo access is allowed, each time you open Sofar (at most once every six hours) it looks for new geotagged photos and shows you a card with what it found; nothing is added until you open it, review the places and confirm. You can turn this off in Settings with Find places in new photos. Your photo app's own syncing is separate from Sofar.

Place names

Unless you turn on Place names offline in Settings, Sofar asks your phone's built-in geocoder, provided by your phone's maker and usually Google, for a street and city name. The geocoder receives the exact coordinates of a place. It is asked for each new place the next time Sofar opens or comes back to the screen, when you fill in missing names from Data health, once for an older place whose automatic name Sofar can now improve, when you review places found in your photos, when you move the map to pick a spot for a past place, and, when Photon returns nothing for a search, when you type in the search field for adding a past place, where it receives the text you typed. Your phone's maker may use a network connection to answer, under its own privacy policy. Turning on Place names offline stops every new naming request; places then keep their coordinates and their country, which Sofar resolves from map data bundled inside the app. With it on, search asks only your own atlas and the list of countries built into the app. It cannot undo a request that was already made.

Nearby place suggestions use OpenStreetMap data. When you log a new place, or log again at a place that still has its automatic name, Sofar sends that place's coordinate, rounded to about 100 metres, over an encrypted connection to Photon, a free public service run by komoot at photon.komoot.io, and can show you a few nearby place names to choose from. It does the same when you rename a place found in your photos, for up to 20 places each time you review. When you search for a place to add by hand, Sofar also sends the words you type to Photon over the same encrypted connection, once you have typed three characters, and shows matching countries, cities and places. It sends no coordinate with a search. The request carries the app's name and version and a contact address so the service can identify the app, and the app's language when it is English, French or German. Like any internet request, the service can see your phone's network address and the time. It carries no account or device identifier. Rounding makes the coordinate less precise; it does not make the request anonymous, and what Photon keeps is governed by komoot's own terms. Sofar keeps the answers on your phone for up to thirty days, sends no request while Place names offline is on, and deletes the saved answers when you turn it on. It never applies a suggestion without your tap. Map data: OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL, openstreetmap.org/copyright.

Purchases

Sofar offers a single optional one-time purchase, Sofar Complete. Google Play processes the payment; Sofar never receives, sees or stores your card or bank details. When the app starts, Sofar asks Google Play whether the store is available, for the product's current price, and whether this Google account has bought it, so a purchase can be restored and its confirmation to Google Play completed. Sofar receives the purchase status. The app may also ask Google Play to show its review prompt, at most three times ever. Google Play's billing library, which Sofar uses for the purchase, may also send Google technical diagnostics about the purchase screen, such as your device model, country and network type, under Google's own privacy policy. Sofar does not receive or use this data.

Widget and quick settings tile

If you add Sofar's home-screen widget, it shows your latest place and a summary of your atlas on your home screen, where anyone who can see your screen can read it. The widget's Log button and the quick settings tile open Sofar and log where you are, just like tapping Log in the app.

Notifications

Every notification Sofar shows is created on your phone by Sofar itself, from checks it runs from time to time, including while the app is closed, and from Stays and Memory echo when you turn them on. Nothing is pushed from a server.

Sharing and feedback

When you share a card, export a file or send feedback, Android hands the content to an app you choose, and that app and its recipient handle it under their own policies. Unless you turn off Home private in shares in Settings, share cards do not name or pin the places Sofar treats as your home; exports and backups always contain everything. Feedback includes the app version and the Android version, plus anything you add.

Keeping and deleting your data

Your atlas stays on your phone until you remove it, and you can see, correct, export and delete it yourself. To remove everything Sofar holds on your phone, uninstall the app or use Clear storage for Sofar in Android's settings; either one removes its database and its own backup copies. Inside the app, Move to trash takes a visit out of your atlas, but the visit is kept, in the app and in its backups, so you can restore it later. Removing an imported photo under Imported photos in Settings deletes the visit it created, and the place too when that was its last visit. Sofar's own backup copies can still hold something you removed until they are replaced. Files you exported or shared, and copies in a backup folder you chose, are yours: Sofar cannot delete them, so delete them where you keep them.

Children

Sofar is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from them. It has no account, no advertising and no analytics.

Third parties

Sofar contains no third-party analytics, advertising, crash reporting or tracking SDKs. The third parties that can be involved are: Google Play, which distributes the app, processes the optional purchase and shows the review prompt; Google Play services, which watches your Memory echo places and provides the activity recognition and the location reading for Stays when you turn those on; komoot's Photon service, for nearby place names, which receives a rounded coordinate as described under Place names, and for place search, which receives the words you type there; your phone's geocoder service, for street and city names; the storage provider behind a backup folder you choose; and any app you share a file or feedback with. Map data by OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.
